06

The four numbers, as at 14 August 2026

Certified, invoiced, received, and held. In conversation all four are "the money"; in the platform they are four different figures with three meaningful gaps between them. A reader who cannot say which is which will misread every commercial screen.

All four figures are net of VAT

The commercial side of the platform works in net amounts — the contract, the BOQ, the certificates, and the bar chart above. Output VAT of 5% is added when the invoice is raised, so the amount the client is actually billed is higher: certificate 04's $960,575 becomes an invoice of $1,008,603.75. Section 09 picks the story up from there.

09

Into the books — every event, and the journal it writes

Nothing above this section is accounting. A bill of quantities, a certificate, a purchase order — those are operational records of what was agreed and what was done. Accounting is what the company writes down as a consequence, and it happens at far fewer moments than newcomers expect.

Just enough double entry

Every journal entry has at least two lines and they must sum to zero: total debits equal total credits. A debit increases an asset or an expense; a credit increases a liability, equity, or revenue. That is the whole rule. If a journal does not balance the platform refuses it — IMBALANCED_JOURNAL is classed as a platform bug that must never reach Finance.

The chart of accounts is the list of buckets those lines land in. These are the ones Hodan Heights touches, taken from the accounting event catalog:

VAT on this job

Output VAT is 5%, added on top of the certified amount when the client invoice is raised. Input VAT is non-recoverable for ACCO, so a supplier bill posts its gross amount straight to the expense account with no separate VAT line — the VAT is retained on the bill line for the VAT return only. Every figure in sections 01–08 of this sheet is stated net of VAT, which is how the commercial side works; the journals below are the first place VAT appears.

The eight moments that matter

Open question · two things the catalog does not have

Retention has no account. The client invoice is raised for the net amount, so the $282,150 of retention held has never been recognised as revenue and appears nowhere on the balance sheet. Under most construction accounting it would sit as a retention receivable. Either the catalog is missing an event, or ACCO deliberately recognises revenue only on the claimable amount — a real policy question, not a documentation gap.

Goods received but not invoiced has no event. Account 60200 Accrued Expense exists in the chart, but nothing writes to it from a goods receipt. Between 2 July and 25 July, $334,800 of concrete was on site, consumed, and invisible to the general ledger. It was only ever in the commitment ledger.

Which date a posting takes

Correct by reversal, never by edit

A posted journal is immutable. A mistake is fixed by posting a REVERSAL that negates every line — carrying the original's accounting date — and then a fresh correct entry. The audit trail therefore shows the error, the undoing, and the fix, in that order. Nothing is ever quietly rewritten, which is the property that makes the ledger worth trusting at all.

11

How to use this sheet

The contract with every chapter

Read this sheet before drawing anything, and take every number from it. If a chapter needs a figure that is not here — a fifth certificate, a variation order, a second supplier — it is added here first, checked for consistency against everything above, and only then used. A number invented locally inside one chapter is how the set stops compounding.

All figures are USDbase currency
Confirmed from the code — the accounting module's examples and opening-balance defaults are all USD. No multi-currency case is drawn on this sheet; if a chapter needs one, add an explicit foreign-currency supplier here rather than improvising a rate.
Everything is inventedno real ACCO data
No real client, project, person, or figure from ASAS CONSTRUCTION appears here. Names were chosen to be plausible for the market ACCO works in and nothing more.
Two numbers are inheritedcontinuity with the System Map
The already-published Rukna System Map uses 2,400 m³ of concrete at 185/m³ and the claim-versus-certify example 1,800 claimed, 1,750 agreed. Both are preserved exactly, as BOQ line 03.01.001 and its certificate 04 entry, so a reader arriving from that page recognises the numbers. ConsequenceDo not renumber 03.01.001. Anything else on this sheet can be revised; that line and that variance are load-bearing across two documents.
The arithmetic is checkedand must stay checked
Section totals sum to $14,850,000. The 03.01 leaves sum to $2,860,000. The waterfall lands on $960,575. Certified minus deductions equals invoiced. If a chapter changes a figure and the sums stop closing, the chapter is wrong, not the sheet.
Open questions carried by this sheet

1 — Retention is not in the books. The client invoice posts the net amount, so $282,150 of retention held is neither revenue nor an asset anywhere in the ledger. Missing event, or deliberate policy? See section 09.

2 — Goods received but not invoiced writes nothing. Account 60200 Accrued Expense exists and nothing reaches it from a goods receipt. See section 09.

3 — certifiedQuantity semantics. Read as a period figure; see the callout in section 05.

4 — retentionCap basis. Read as a rate applied to contract value, giving a $519,750 ceiling. If the code instead caps against cumulative certified, the cap never binds and the second chart in section 04 is wrong.

5 — Does the commitment ledger record net or gross? This sheet assumes the commitment ledger tracks the net purchase-order value ($334,800) while the general ledger takes the VAT-inclusive gross ($351,540), because ACCO cannot reclaim input VAT. If both are net, the two books disagree by the VAT on every package.
